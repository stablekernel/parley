## Synthesis (the agent's current read)
**My read: this is a stronger idea than its surface weirdness suggests, because the name, the
brand, the core mechanic, and the development method are all the *same* idea — "IDK."** A game
that withholds its rules, where discovery *is* play; shaped per-player by identity so no two
games match; and (the radical version) built by people who don't fully know the rules either,
because building and playing may be one act. The honest answer to "how do you play?" is the
product's name.

The concept stands on proven ground with one genuinely novel leg. Mechanical cousins —
**Understand**, tabletop **Zendo** (infer the hidden rule from examples), and
**Outer Wilds / Tunic / The Witness / Animal Well** (knowledge *is* progression) — prove the
"figure out the rules" genre works. None do the **per-player-seeded** twist; that's the
novelty, and also where the risk concentrates.

**The unifying spine I'd hand the next person: one master dial — intimacy vs. scale.** Most of
Kevin's apparent contradictions are downstream of a single knob. Dial *left* = an intimate cult
of 2–3 builder-players using real identities, maybe never published, where the experiment is
the artifact. Dial *right* = a spreading phenomenon where more builders = more variety = more
fun, identity gating entry through a deliberately-grown circle of trust. Turning that one knob
makes the other pieces (PII, retention, growth cadence, multiplayer) fall into place — which is
the antidote to "this is too much to parse."

**Reasoning trail (what moved this read):**
- It started as "intriguing but maybe too clever." The inflection was realizing the
  agentic-coding dev conceit, the build-by-playing idea, and the "IDK" brand are *one* thread —
  *building = playing*. That collapsed three loose pieces into a thesis and made the whole thing
  feel coherent rather than gimmicky.
- The second mover was Kevin *flipping* the PII crux: I'd treated real personal info as a
  privacy liability to engineer around; he reframed it as the *feature* (you can't play as
  someone else → enforced one-person-one-experience → trust circle). That reframe is what
  surfaced the intimacy/scale dial as the real organizing axis.
- I deliberately did **not** resolve the intimacy-vs-scale tension; Kevin asked that opposing
  ideas be preserved as live forks, so my synthesis names the dial rather than picking a setting.
